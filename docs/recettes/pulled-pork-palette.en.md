## 1. Overview

Oven-baked pulled pork made from semi-salted bone-in pork shoulder, desalted the day before then cooked long and slow: the 150 °C cook melts the collagen, then the meat is deboned, shredded and coated again in its own juices and a homemade BBQ sauce. The sauce is made separately and only mixed in at the shredding stage, so it can be dosed by use (burgers, pasta). Desalting is a must with semi-salted meat, otherwise the result is too salty once the juices reduce. Makes 8 to 10 servings. Desalting: 12 hours. Preparation: 20 minutes. Cooking: 4 h 30. Resting: 30 minutes.

## 2. Ingredients

### Meat

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Semi-salted bone-in pork shoulder | 2 x 800 g (1.6 kg) |
| Neutral oil | 1 tbsp |
| Chicken or vegetable stock | 250 ml |

### Dry rub

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Smoked paprika | 2 tbsp |
| Brown sugar | 2 tbsp |
| Fine salt | 1 tsp |
| Ground black pepper | 1 tsp |
| Garlic powder | 1 tsp |
| Onion powder | 1 tsp |
| Ground cumin | 1 tsp |

The salt in the rub is deliberately low: the semi-salted shoulder stays salted to the core after desalting and completes the seasoning.

### Homemade BBQ sauce

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Ketchup | 200 g |
| Maple syrup or brown sugar | 60 g |
| Cider vinegar | 3 tbsp |
| Worcestershire sauce | 1 tbsp |
| Mustard | 1 tsp |
| Smoked paprika | 1 tsp |
| Garlic powder | 1 tsp |
| Mild chili or a pinch of cayenne | (optional) |

## 3. Equipment

- Cast iron Dutch oven with lid (or deep roasting dish plus foil)
- Large container for desalting
- Oven probe or probe thermometer
- Brush or hands to massage the rub in
- 2 forks or claws for shredding
- Large board or dish for shredding
- Small saucepan for the sauce
- Baking tray for the burgers
- 2 glass meal-prep boxes for leftovers

## 4. Method

### Desalting (the day before)

1. Rinse the pork shoulders under cold water.
2. Submerge them in a large covered container with at least 3 litres of cold water.
3. Desalt for 12 h in the refrigerator, changing the water once after 4 to 6 h.
4. Drain and pat the shoulders dry, take them out of the fridge 1 h before cooking.

Express version, only for a thin cut: change the cold water every 30 minutes for 2 h.

### Dry rub

1. Mix all the rub ingredients in a bowl.
2. Rub each shoulder evenly with 1 tbsp oil then half the rub on all sides.
3. Rest for 30 minutes at room temperature (or go straight to cooking if the desalting finished the day before).

### Cooking

1. Preheat the oven to 150 °C.
2. Pour the stock into the Dutch oven, place the two shoulders fat side up.
3. Bake covered for 3 h.
4. Check the liquid level: about 1 cm should remain at the bottom, add 100 ml of water if needed.
5. Continue cooking uncovered for 1 h to 1 h 15, until a probe inserted into the muscle slides in without resistance and the internal temperature reaches 90 to 92 °C.
6. Take out of the oven and rest the meat 30 minutes, covered.

### BBQ sauce

1. During the cook, bring all the sauce ingredients to a gentle simmer in a small saucepan.
2. Simmer for 10 minutes over low heat, stirring, until the sauce coats the spoon. Set aside.

### Shredding and mixing

1. Pull the bones out of the shoulders (they come out on their own at this temperature) and trim away cartilage and hard bits of fat.
2. Shred the meat by hand or with two forks into 2 to 3 cm pieces.
3. Pour the defatted cooking juices over the meat, then add 200 to 300 ml of BBQ sauce and toss gently. Keep the rest of the sauce as a dipping pot.

## 5. Key points

- Desalting is not optional: semi-salted pork releases its salt during the 4 h cook and the reducing juices concentrate it, leaving the meat too salty underneath the sauce.
- The meat must reach at least 90 °C at the core: below that the collagen is not converted and shredding pulls out tough strands instead of threads.
- If the crust browns too fast, lay a sheet of foil on top and lower the oven to 140 °C.
- Defat the cooking juices (the fat rises to the surface, skim it off with a spoon): otherwise the pulled pork turns greasy once cold.
- Do not drown the meat in sauce when shredding: season at the table, the sauce quickly masks the pork flavour.

## 6. Serving and storage

### Burgers (4 burgers)

1. Preheat the oven to 180 °C (or the grill) and split the burger buns in two.
2. Mix 1 tbsp BBQ sauce with 1 tbsp wholegrain mustard, then spread a spoonful on each cut face of the buns.
3. Top each bottom bun with 120 g of hot pulled pork and lay 1 to 2 slices of cheddar over it.
4. Bake the topped buns and the lids on a tray for 3 to 4 minutes, until the cheddar melts and the buns toast slightly.
5. Take the burgers out of the oven, add sliced gherkins (4 to 5 per burger) and a lettuce leaf, cap with the top bun and serve at once.

### Pasta

- Reheat one portion (90 g) in a pan with a little cooking juice, toss with al dente pasta and 2 tbsp of sauce, finish with parmesan.

### Storage

- 4 days in the refrigerator in an airtight box with the juices, or 3 months frozen flat in portion bags.
- Reheating from the fridge: 5 minutes in a pan over medium heat with 1 tbsp of water or juice. Frozen: thaw overnight in the fridge then reheat in a pan.
- Cooking and shredding the day before works fine: the meat tastes even better the next day once reheated.

## 7. Variations

- Replace the stock with 200 ml of dry cider + 50 ml of water for a more fruity note.
- Add 2 tbsp of soy sauce to the BBQ sauce for a salty-umami kick.
- Replace the brown sugar with honey (50 g) in the sauce for less caramel and a looser glaze, better suited to pasta.
