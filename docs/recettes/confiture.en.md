## 1. Overview

A short-cooking fruit jam set with agar agar: the gel forms while cooling, without a long reduction, which keeps the fresh fruit taste. Inverting the jars after filling pasteurises the surface and the lid. Makes three 250 g jars. Preparation: 15 minutes. Cooking: 20 minutes.

## 2. Ingredients

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Fruit (fresh or frozen) | 1 kg |
| Water | 300 ml |
| Agave syrup or maple syrup | 100 g |
| Agar agar | 2 g |

## 3. Equipment

- 1 large saucepan
- 1 skimmer
- 3 x 250 g jars with lids

## 4. Preparation

1. Wash, prepare and cut the fruit into pieces.
2. Heat the fruit with the water in the saucepan over medium heat for 15 to 20 minutes, until the fruit has completely broken down.
3. Rain in the agar agar and boil 2 minutes, stirring: agar agar only gels after boiling.
4. Off the heat, add the agave syrup and stir.
5. Pour straight into the jars and close.
6. Wait 15 minutes.
7. Turn the jars upside down and let them cool at room temperature.

## 5. Key points

- The agar agar must be brought to the boil to activate, otherwise the jam will not set.
- The jam stays liquid while hot: it sets as it cools, and firms up in the fridge.
- Inverting the jars sterilises the lid and the surface: do not skip it.
- With little sugar, the shelf life is shorter than a classic jam.

## 6. Variations

- Blend half the fruit for a smoother jam.
- A vanilla pod or the zest of half a lemon cooked with the fruit.
- A mix of red berries, or apricot and peach in summer.

## 7. Serving and storage

- Open the jars only once completely cooled.
- Opened jars: 1 to 2 weeks in the fridge.
- Unopened jars: 3 to 4 weeks in the fridge, or in a cool dark place otherwise.
